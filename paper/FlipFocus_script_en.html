FlipFocus — 2-Minute Presentation Script

Speaker: Decheng Wang | CHCI 2026 | ~2 min

Slide 1 — Title (5s)

Good morning / afternoon, everyone. I'm Decheng Wang from Shanghai Sanlin High School. Today I'll present FlipFocus — a browser-sensor-based tool for detecting phone-flipping behavior.

Slide 2 — Motivation (18s)

Have you ever noticed — before studying or starting work, you habitually flip your phone face-down? That 2-second gesture may be a self-regulation ritual.

But studying it faces three barriers:

  • First, every experiment requires developing a native app, going through app-store review, and having participants install it — taking weeks. For high-school research, this is simply infeasible.
  • Second, no existing tool is purpose-built for detecting phone flips. Current EMA platforms only do survey sampling; they don't track the physical state of the phone.
  • Third, phone sensors vary enormously across devices. iOS Safari doesn't expose the ambient-light API; some Huawei devices disable the proximity sensor. A fixed sensor-combination approach breaks outright on a large number of devices.

Slide 3 — Core Design Philosophy (15s)

The core idea of FlipFocus: turn sensor diversity from a bug into a feature.

At runtime, it probes which sensors are available on the device and auto-selects a fusion mode. Missing ambient-light sensor? It gracefully degrades to accelerometer-only — the experiment keeps running. The UI shows real-time data quality.

In dual-sensor mode, F1 reaches 0.970. And it's zero-install — participants scan a QR code in their browser and start immediately.

Slide 4 — Three Core Modules (15s)

Module A — Degradation Engine: probes sensor availability and auto-switches fusion modes.

Module B — State Machine: a 3-second confirmation window distinguishes intentional flips from random noise; ±5-second transition periods are excluded; XY-axis stillness is verified.

Module C — Experiment Ecosystem: four-condition within-subjects design paradigm, standardized material kit, and a Python-based end-to-end analysis pipeline. Send one WeChat link to start data collection; run one script to produce publication-ready figures.

Slide 5 — Real-World Findings (20s)

We conducted a real-world validation: 4 participants, 4 conditions, 25-minute sessions. We collected the first-ever complete sensor array of this kind — 14,981 sampling points.

The most surprising finding involved P2: instructed to keep the phone face-up, he spontaneously flipped 6 times, totaling 265 seconds face-down.

One of those flips lasted only 4.9 seconds — he immediately realized "wait, I'm supposed to be face-up" and flipped back. This tells us two things:

  1. Flip behavior is strongly habitual — verbal instructions alone cannot suppress it.
  2. The 4.9-second self-correction confirms that the instruction was present in the participant's awareness — it just wasn't strong enough.

Slide 6 — Self-Reported Focus Analysis (18s)

The self-report questionnaire also yielded interesting results.

Median focus score was highest in the Active-Flip condition — 19.5 points. The Face-Up condition scored lowest — 15.5 points. The Friedman test was significant.

Key comparison: experimenter-flip and face-up showed little difference — only when participants flipped the phone themselves did the focus experience clearly improve. Physical state alone is not enough; the agency of performing the flip may be the critical psychological factor.

Validation: self-report scores correlated with objective accuracy at r = 0.771, p < 0.001. The questionnaire successfully captured genuine focus signals, not subjective bias.

Slide 7 — Comparison with Prior Work (10s)

Compared to existing tools: PIEL Survey requires app installation; browser-sensor studies don't detect flips; lab-based studies lack ecological validity.

FlipFocus is the only platform that checks all four boxes: zero-install, sensor-adaptive, flip-detection, and experiment-ready.

Slide 8 — Four Core Contributions (8s)

  • C1: Adaptive three-tier sensor degradation strategy
  • C2: 3-second confirmation-window state machine, F1 = 0.970
  • C3: Complete experiment ecosystem
  • C4: First complete 25-minute sensor dataset + spontaneous flip behavior discovery + r = 0.771

Slide 9 — Summary (8s)

In one sentence: FlipFocus lowers the deployment barrier for mobile HCI experiments from "develop an app" to "send a link."

Code, materials, and analysis pipeline are all open-source — usable by everyone from high-schoolers to professional researchers.
